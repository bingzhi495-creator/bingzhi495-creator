# Skill: Project-Based Learning Design

## Purpose

Design learning experiences that include a meaningful question, a visible process, evidence, revision, and student presentation.

## Problems This Skill Solves

- Students complete tasks without understanding why they are learning.
- A project looks active but lacks evidence and assessment criteria.
- The learning process is difficult to track or improve.
- Final presentations look polished, but students cannot clearly explain their thinking.

## Workflow

1. Create a driving question that is authentic, open enough for inquiry, and narrow enough to complete.
2. Define the final product, presentation, or learning artifact students will produce.
3. Break the project into research, planning, practice, testing, presentation, and reflection stages.
4. Add checkpoints and evidence requirements for each stage.
5. Use rubrics to define quality for process, product, and communication.
6. Build in peer feedback, teacher feedback, and student revision.

## Typical Outputs

- Driving question
- Project brief
- Stage checklist
- Process journal template
- Presentation rubric
- Reflection prompts

## Public Boundary

This skill shares general PBL design methods. It does not disclose real student work, identifiable learner information, competition-sensitive materials, or commercial teaching services.
